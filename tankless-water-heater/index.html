Is Tankless Right for Your House?

A tankless water heater heats water as you use it, instead of keeping a tank hot all day. That can mean steady hot water for long showers and a smaller footprint on the wall. It also means the unit has to produce a lot of heat quickly, so the house must support it.

The questions that decide it are practical. Is the gas line big enough for the appliance? Can the unit vent where it needs to? How many fixtures run at once? In a Nashua winter, incoming water is cold, and the unit has to raise that temperature a long way, which affects how many fixtures one unit can serve at a time. We check all of this before we recommend one.

Tank or Tankless

Neither is the better choice for every house. Our water heater installation page covers how we plan either one.

Tank water heater

  • Lower upfront cost in most cases
  • Simple, familiar technology
  • Stores hot water, which can run out
  • Takes floor space

Tankless water heater

  • Hot water on demand
  • Wall mounted, smaller footprint
  • Needs correct gas line and venting
  • Needs periodic descaling

Tankless Installation Requirements

  • ✓Gas supply: tankless units draw a lot of gas at once, and an undersized line starves the appliance.
  • ✓Venting: tankless units use specific vent materials and routes, often through a sidewall.
  • ✓Condensate: condensing models need a drain for acidic condensate.
  • ✓Water supply: the unit needs adequate flow and pressure and often an isolation valve set for flushing.
  • ✓Electrical: most units need a power outlet for controls and fans.

The City of Nashua plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code, caps delivered water temperature for tankless units and calls for tempering protection. We set the unit to meet that and explain the setting to you. We also ask the Department of Building Safety whether your project needs a permit.

Descaling and Maintenance

Tankless units need regular flushing to keep the heat exchanger clear. Nashua tap water reported by Pennichuck is on the soft side, at about 21 ppm hardness, so scale builds more slowly than in many parts of the country. Hudson and some private wells run harder, and a flush schedule that suits one address may not suit another. We set the schedule based on your water.

Isolation valves

These let us flush the unit without removing it. We install them on new units and add them to older ones.

Inlet filter

A clogged filter reduces flow. We clean it during every service.

Error codes

Codes point to ignition, venting, flow or temperature faults. We read the code and fix the cause.

Annual service

A yearly visit catches small problems before the heat exchanger suffers.

Tankless Repair

We repair tankless units that run cold, shut off mid shower, throw error codes or cycle on and off. Many problems come from clogged filters, scale, failed igniters, venting issues or flow sensors. If a unit is beyond repair, we say so and explain replacement options. For an emergency with no hot water at all, call our 24/7 line and we will triage by phone.

Venting and Gas Safety

Gas tankless units move a lot of exhaust and need correct venting and gas line sizing. Installation follows the manufacturer instructions and NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and a carbon monoxide alarm nearby is a smart precaution.
