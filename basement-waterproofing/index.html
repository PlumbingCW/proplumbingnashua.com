Basement Waterproofing in Nashua, NH

A wet basement has a cause. We find where the water comes from, explain the options and install drainage and pump systems that move it out of the house.

Where Basement Water Comes From

Groundwater rising

A high water table pushes water up through the slab or the cove joint where the wall meets the floor.

Surface water

Poor grading, clogged gutters and short downspouts send water against the foundation.

Cracks and joints

Cracks in a poured wall or a failing joint let water in under pressure.

Block wall seepage

Hollow block walls can fill with water and weep at the bottom.

Plumbing leaks

A broken supply line or a failing drain can look like a wet wall. We check this first.

Condensation

Humid air on cold walls leaves damp patches that are not a leak.

What We Do About It

We start with the cheapest cause and work toward the expensive one. Check the gutters and grading, look for plumbing leaks, and then consider interior drainage if the water enters through the floor or wall base.

  • ✓Interior perimeter drain that collects water at the footing and sends it to a sump pit.
  • ✓Sump pump with a backup, sized to the inflow.
  • ✓Wall and floor sealing where it helps, along with drainage that handles the water.
  • ✓Advice on gutters, downspouts and grading, which are often the cheapest fix.

Interior and Exterior Approaches

Waterproofing approaches compared
ApproachWhat it doesTradeoff
Interior drain and sumpCollects water at the footing and pumps it outWorks from inside, less excavation
Exterior waterproofingSeals and drains the outside of the foundation wallMost thorough, requires excavation
Grading and guttersMoves surface water awayLow cost, only solves surface water
Crack injectionSeals a specific crackTargets one leak, not groundwater

Exterior waterproofing is a larger project, often handled by foundation contractors. We tell you when that is the right answer and when interior drainage will do.

Water Table and Seasons in Nashua

Nashua has spring snowmelt, heavy rain and homes close to rivers and Salmon Brook. Many basements are dry for most of the year and wet for a few weeks, which makes it harder to know what is happening. If the water comes after storms, grading and gutters are the first suspects. If it comes in spring with no rain, groundwater and snowmelt are likely.

Older homes in Downtown, French Hill and the Tree Streets may have stone or brick foundations that were never meant to be waterproof, and wet basements are common. We tell you honestly what is reasonable to expect from an old foundation.

Keep Water Off Your Septic and Well Systems

In Hollis and other private septic areas, basement drainage must never connect to the septic tank. We route water to daylight or a safe discharge. We also check that waterproofing work does not disturb a well line or a septic line near the foundation. Call before a contractor digs near either.

What a Visit Looks Like

We walk the basement with you and ask when the water appears: after rain, in spring, or all the time. We look at the cove joint, the wall base, floor cracks, the sump pit if there is one and the outside grading. We also check the plumbing, because a failed supply line or a leaking drain can mimic a foundation leak, and fixing the wrong problem wastes money.

You leave with a plain explanation of the likely cause, the options in order from cheapest to most involved and a clear price before work starts for the option you choose. If the best answer is a gutter fix you can do yourself, we say so.
