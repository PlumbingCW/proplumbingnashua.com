Leaking Garbage Disposal Repair for Nashua, NH Homes

Where a garbage disposal leaks tells you what is wrong. A leak at the top, under the sink flange, is usually a failed seal and is fixable. A leak at the drain pipe or dishwasher connection is a loose clamp or gasket. A leak from the side or bottom of the body means the housing or an internal seal has failed, and the unit should be replaced.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published October 27, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

Find the Source Before You Fix

Dry the unit and the area around it, then run water into the sink and watch. Place a dry paper towel under each joint. The towel that gets wet first points to the leak. Do this with the disposal off at the wall switch and the breaker if you need to touch the unit.

A leak that only shows when the disposal runs points to an internal seal. A leak that shows when water drains from the sink points to a flange or a drain connection. A leak only when the dishwasher drains points to the dishwasher hose.

When to Replace Instead

A cracked housing cannot be repaired. A leak from the bottom or side almost always means the unit is done. An old disposal with several repairs is a candidate too. Replacement takes about the same time as a complicated repair, and the new unit comes with new seals and a mounting kit.

If your sink also drains slowly, the cause may be farther down. Our drain cleaning page explains when the line needs more than a disposal fix.

Local Note: Disposals on Municipal Sewer and Septic

Nashua is on municipal sewer, according to the state community profile, so a disposal sends ground food waste to the city plant. That is allowed, but grease is a problem in any system. In Hollis, where homes use private septic according to the same profile, a disposal adds solids to the tank and can shorten the time between pumpings. Ask your septic contractor before you add one.

We repair and install disposals in Hudson and Merrimack too. The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code with local amendments, and our installations follow it.

Keeping a Disposal Healthy

Run cold water before, during and after use. Grind small amounts at a time. Keep grease, bones, fibrous scraps and large amounts of starch out. Clean it with ice and citrus peel now and then to knock off residue. A disposal that has been well treated lasts years, and one that is abused fails early.

How to Replace the Flange Seal

A leak at the top of the disposal, where it meets the sink, is a failed seal. Fixing it takes about an hour. Turn off the power, disconnect the drain and dishwasher hose, loosen the mounting ring and lower the disposal. Remove the old flange and scrape off the old putty. Roll fresh plumber putty into a rope, set it under the new flange lip, press the flange into the sink, and tighten the mounting assembly from below.

Wipe off the squeezed out putty, reattach the disposal and run water to test. If the leak returns, the sink opening may be damaged or the flange may be cracked, and a replacement flange or unit is needed.

Protect the Cabinet Floor

A slow leak under a sink does damage you cannot see. Particleboard cabinet floors swell and crumble when wet, and mold grows on the underside. Check under your sink every few months with a flashlight. Look for stains, a musty smell or a swollen cabinet floor. A cheap leak alarm under the sink gives a warning before the damage spreads.

If water has been leaking for a while, dry the cabinet and replace the floor if it has softened. Our faucet repair page also covers supply line leaks that can look like a disposal leak.

Before You Buy a New Unit

If the old unit has to go, choose a replacement that fits your sink and your use. Horsepower affects grinding power and price, and a bigger motor is quieter and handles tougher scraps. Stainless steel grinding parts last longer than galvanized ones. Check the mounting type, because many units use a quick-lock mount that lets you swap without disturbing the sink flange. Measure the space under the sink, because tall units may not fit above a deep trap.

Ask your installer to reuse the flange only if it is in good condition. A new unit on an old flange with a failed seal will leak again. Also confirm that the electrical outlet or hard wire connection is sound, and that the dishwasher knockout plug is removed if a dishwasher is connected.

The Short Version

Find the leak by drying the area and watching where water first appears. Top and connection leaks are repairable. A leak from the body means replacement. Protect the cabinet floor, and call us if you are not sure which kind of leak you have.
