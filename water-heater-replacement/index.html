When a Tank Fails, This Is What to Do

Our 24/7 line is open, and replacement is one of the calls we handle most at night. If water is coming from the tank, close the cold water valve on top, shut off the power or gas, and open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure. Then call so we can talk through the tank size, fuel type and the room it sits in.

We offer same-day replacement appointments when the schedule allows, and we will tell you plainly if a specific model needs ordering. If the tank is sound and only one part has failed, we will say so and send you to our water heater repair page instead of selling you a new unit.

Signs It Is Time to Replace, Not Repair

Leak from the shell

Water leaking from the tank body means the steel has failed. No fitting repair fixes that.

Rust at the base

Rust streaks, flaking metal and damp insulation near the bottom are signs of a tank on its way out.

Repeated repairs

When the second and third repair arrive within a short time, the next failure is usually close behind.

Not keeping up

A tank that runs out of hot water for a household that has not changed has probably lost capacity to sediment.

Choosing the Right Replacement

The best replacement is not always the same size and fuel as the old one. We size the tank to your household and look at what is possible in the room. A forty gallon tank that served two people may be too small once a family grows. A basement installation with a good flue is easy, while a closet unit with tight clearances may limit your choices.

  • ✓Gas tank: familiar, quick recovery, needs a safe flue and gas connection.
  • ✓Electric tank: simple installation, no flue, higher running cost in many homes.
  • ✓Hybrid heat pump: efficient, needs space and airflow around the unit.
  • ✓Tankless: endless hot water on demand, needs adequate gas supply or electrical capacity. See our tankless water heater page.

We explain the trade-offs in plain terms and let you choose. Where a unit has to meet a local requirement, we tell you which one.

What Happens on Replacement Day

1

Shut down and drain

We shut off fuel and water and drain the old tank safely.

2

Remove the old unit

The old tank comes out of the house and we clean the area around the base.

3

Set and connect

The new tank is set, connected to water, gas or power, and fitted with a new relief valve and discharge pipe.

4

Test and explain

We fire the unit, check for leaks and combustion safety where it applies, and show you the shutoffs and settings.

We set the temperature to what the manufacturer and local code call for. The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code with local amendments, and we ask the Department of Building Safety whether a permit is needed for your specific job.

What Shapes Replacement Price

The price of a replacement is driven by the unit you choose and the work around it. We provide a clear price before work starts.

Tank typeA standard tank, a hybrid heat pump unit and a tankless unit differ widely in cost and in the work they need.
Venting and gasA new flue, a longer gas run or a larger gas line adds labor and materials.
Code itemsExpansion tanks, tempering valves and discharge piping depend on the home and the code.
AccessA finished basement, a tight closet or stairs add time to removal and set.

We never guess at the price before we see the install. Give us the tank details and we will tell you what to expect.

Safety Steps Before Service

A new heater has to be vented, supported and drained correctly. Gas models follow NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and every tank needs a relief valve piped to a safe discharge point. The local building office tells you whether a permit applies.

  • ✓Clear a path to the heater and keep pets out of the work area.
  • ✓Turn off the gas valve if you smell gas and leave the house before you call.
  • ✓Know where your main water shutoff is so we can confirm it works before we start.
