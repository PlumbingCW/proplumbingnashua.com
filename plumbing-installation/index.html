Planning a Remodel or Addition

Plumbing decisions are easiest before walls close. If you are adding a bathroom, moving a kitchen sink or finishing a basement, talk to us during design. Drain slope, vent routing and shutoff access are cheap to plan and expensive to change.

We look at where the supply and drain lines run now, what the house can support and what the new layout needs. We explain which parts of the job need a permit and inspection. Where a job needs a repair first, we say so, and our repiping and sewer line repair pages cover the most common ones.

Gas Appliance Safety

Gas work is not a place for guesses. If you smell gas, leave the building, do not touch switches or phones inside, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Then call us once the utility says the area is safe. We do not work on a line that smells of gas until the utility has cleared it.

In New Hampshire, plumbers and gas fitters are licensed through the Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board. We recommend you ask any contractor for license details and verify them with the board. We leak test every gas connection we make and tell you what we tested.

Backflow Prevention

Backflow is the reversal of water flow in a plumbing system, which can pull contaminated water into the drinking supply. Devices such as vacuum breakers and testable assemblies prevent it. They are common on irrigation systems, boilers, laundry hookups and commercial equipment.

Some assemblies must be tested on a schedule, and the town or the water utility can require it. We install and repair backflow devices and coordinate testing. Ask us what your property needs, and see our commercial plumbing page for business properties.

Codes, Permits and Inspection

The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code under the New Hampshire State Building Code, with local amendments. That covers pipe materials, venting, water heater safety and more. Whether your job needs a permit depends on what you are doing and where. We ask the Department of Building Safety so you hear it from the source.

In Hudson, plumbing permits go through the Fire Department Inspectional Services Division, and inspections are required at stages such as under-slab, rough-in and final. We schedule them with you.

Installation in Nashua Homes

Older Nashua homes have plaster walls, tight stud bays and original drain lines, so adding a bathroom takes planning. Newer homes in South Nashua and off Amherst Street are easier to reach but still need correct venting. We also work in Hudson, Merrimack and Hollis.

Codes and Permits

New work follows the plumbing code adopted for your address. The Uniform Plumbing Code and International Plumbing Code are the two model codes in use across the country. Ask the Nashua building office which edition and permit rules apply before work begins, and we will build to that standard.
