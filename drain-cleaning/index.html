If a Drain Is Backing Up Right Now

Our 24/7 line is open, so call before the water reaches the floor. While you wait for the plumber, stop running every fixture in the house, because anything you flush or drain keeps feeding the blockage and can push sewage out of the lowest opening, usually a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub.

If only one sink is slow, a few quiet hours rarely hurt anything. If the toilet gurgles when the washer drains, or two or more fixtures back up together, treat it as a main line problem and call at once, even at night. A night or weekend call is routine for us, and the first question is always the same: which drains are affected, and in what order did they slow down.

Where the Clog Usually Sits

Where the blockage lives tells us which tool to bring. A slow bathroom sink with a healthy toilet points to a trap or the short branch behind the wall. A slow tub and shower on the same floor points to the branch line that serves the bathroom group. A backup in the lowest fixture of the house, with gurgling upstairs, points to the main drain leaving the building.

One fixture only

Usually hair and soap in the trap, grease in a kitchen line, or a pop-up stopper that has collected debris. A hand auger or small cable machine reaches it.

One bathroom group

Tub, sink and toilet on the same branch slow together. The blockage sits beyond the individual traps, so we run a larger cable through the branch.

Whole house

Every fixture slows or gurgles, and floor drains can overflow. That is the main building drain, and it often needs a camera look after the cable.

Comes back in weeks

Repeat clogs mean something is still in the pipe: roots, a belly, scale, or grease that a cable only punched through. Jetting or a camera inspection ends the cycle.

How We Clear a Drain

1

Ask and locate

We confirm which fixtures are slow and test the nearest cleanout so we know where to enter the line.

2

Cable the line

A cable machine with the right cutting head runs through the blockage and restores flow.

3

Check the result

We run water through the line to confirm it drains, not just that the cable passed.

4

Recommend next steps

If the pipe looks damaged or the clog returns, we say so and offer a camera inspection or jetting.

We also tell you when a drain does not need a machine. A pop-up stopper or a loose trap seal is a five-minute fix, and calling it anything bigger would be wrong.

Drains in Nashua Homes

Housing in Nashua spans several eras, from older homes near downtown to newer subdivisions. A drain line in any home can narrow, sag or collect grease and paper at a fitting, so we look at the line instead of guessing from the address. Neighborhoods such as the Tree Streets and French Hill have older housing documented by local sources, which is a reason to inspect, not a reason to assume a problem.

The city is on municipal sewer, and the wastewater plant treats what leaves the house, so the blockage you are dealing with is almost always on your side of the property line. Your own drain is your responsibility, which is why a clean cable pass and a camera look at a repeat problem make sense. If the clog sits where your lateral meets the street, we point that out so you can contact the city.

Cable Snaking or Hydro Jetting?

Most calls start with a cable. When a drain keeps coming back, hydro jetting cleans the whole pipe instead of poking one hole through it, and a sewer inspection shows us what the pipe actually looks like.

Cable snaking

  • Best for a single stoppage in a trap or short branch
  • Fast and low impact on older pipe
  • Leaves some grease film on the wall

Hydro jetting

  • Scours the full pipe wall with high pressure water
  • Better for grease, scale and roots
  • Used after a camera look confirms the pipe is sound
