Plumber in the South End, Nashua, NH

The South End lies south of downtown Nashua, west of Main Street. Its housing mixes single-family and multi-family homes, with some very old houses near Kinsley Street and many mid-century homes, and its parks and brooks make basement water a fair thing to plan for.

Where the South End Is

A homes.com neighborhood guide describes the South End as lying west of Main Street and south of the downtown shops. It does not give formal boundaries. The guide mentions Fields Grove Park, a marshy wetland with trails, along with Sandy Pond park, the Rotary Pool and Fairgrounds Elementary and Middle schools off Cleveland Street.

What the Homes Are Like

The guide describes a mix of single-family and multi-family homes on modest lots. It places homes near Kinsley Street at 1900 or earlier and says most homes date from the mid-twentieth century, including ranches, bungalows, Cape Cods and Colonial-inspired two-story houses from roughly the 1950s to the 1970s. It also notes newer Colonial-style homes from 2010 onward.

With such a spread of ages, we do not assume what a given house has. We look at the supply lines, the drains, the water heater and the basement, and we explain what we find before suggesting any work.

Water Near Wetlands and Brooks

The same guide notes that homes near wetlands, Hassells Brook or Salmon Brook Main Street Pond may see wetter conditions or overflow in heavy rain. That is general information, not a promise about any one house. If your basement takes on water, tell us where and when, since the fix may be drainage, a sump pump or something outside the plumbing.

Basement and Everyday Plumbing

  • ✓Test your sump pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit before heavy rain.
  • ✓Check that the discharge pipe runs away from the foundation.
  • ✓Know where your main water shutoff is.
  • ✓Look at your water heater for rust, pooling water or a wet floor.
