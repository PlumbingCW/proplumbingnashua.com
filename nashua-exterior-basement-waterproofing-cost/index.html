Exterior Basement Waterproofing Cost in Nashua, NH

Exterior basement waterproofing is priced by the foot of foundation wall and the difficulty of digging beside it, not by a flat rate. The biggest cost drivers are how deep and how long the wall is, what sits against it such as decks, patios and landscaping, whether the soil is rocky and whether drain tile and a sump are included. A fair quote shows each of these separately.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published October 9, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

What Exterior Waterproofing Is

Exterior waterproofing means digging down along the outside of the foundation wall to the footing, cleaning the wall, applying a waterproof membrane or coating, laying drain tile at the footing and backfilling with gravel and soil. The aim is to stop water before it touches the wall and carry it away from the house.

It is the most thorough approach and also the most disruptive. It makes sense when the wall is cracked, when water pressure against the wall is high or when interior drainage alone will not do. It is usually a foundation contractor project, and we advise on the plumbing side, including sump pumps and discharge.

What Drives the Price

Wall length and depthCost scales with how much wall is exposed. A deep basement wall takes more digging and more material than a shallow one.
AccessA narrow side yard that excavators cannot enter means hand digging, which adds labor.
What is in the wayDecks, patios, stairs, steps, mature shrubs and utility lines all need to be moved or protected.
Soil and ledgeRocky New England soil and ledge slow the dig. Clay holds water and raises the need for drainage.
Drain tile and dischargeNew drain tile needs a place to send water, either a sump pit or a daylight outlet.
RestorationBackfill, grading, sod and replanting are often priced separately.

We do not publish a dollar figure here because the numbers vary widely by house and a number copied from a national site would mislead you. Ask for a written quote that breaks out excavation, membrane, drain tile, sump work and restoration.

Local Note: Soil, Water and Rules Around Nashua

Nashua sits between the Merrimack and Nashua rivers, with Salmon Brook crossing the city. Basements near those waters can see higher groundwater in spring. Digging beside a foundation also means checking for buried utilities, and New Hampshire runs the 811 Dig Safe system for that. Ask the City of Nashua Department of Building Safety whether exterior excavation work needs a permit at your address.

In Hollis, where homes have private wells and septic, any excavation near the foundation must avoid the well line and the septic pipe. Read our Hollis page for how we handle that, and note that sump discharge must never enter a septic system.

Questions to Ask Any Contractor

  • ✓What caused the water, and how do you know?
  • ✓Will the work include new drain tile and where does it discharge?
  • ✓How will you protect the footing and the foundation wall?
  • ✓What happens if you hit ledge?
  • ✓Is there a written scope, a start date and an end date?
  • ✓Who handles permits and inspections?

A contractor who cannot say where the water goes is not ready to dig. For the pump side, see our sump pump page.

Cheaper Fixes to Try First

Before you spend on a large project, check four things. Clean the gutters, because overflow lands next to the wall. Extend downspouts several feet away. Regrade so the ground slopes away from the house. Look for a plumbing leak, because a failing line can look like a wet wall. Each costs little and may solve the problem.

Reading a Quote Line by Line

A good quote reads like a plan. It names the walls to be treated, the depth of excavation, the membrane or coating, the drain tile and its outlet, the gravel and the way soil will be backfilled. A vague quote that lists a single number for the whole house leaves you no way to compare bids or to see what is missing.

  • ✓Scope by wall: which walls are included and how many feet of each.
  • ✓Excavation: depth, equipment and what happens to the soil.
  • ✓Wall preparation: cleaning, crack repair and parging.
  • ✓Membrane or coating: material, thickness and brand.
  • ✓Drain tile and outlet: where the water goes and how it is protected from freezing.
  • ✓Backfill, grading and restoration: what is included and what is not.
  • ✓Warranty terms in writing from the contractor and the manufacturer.

Why Two Quotes for the Same House Can Differ

Contractors price risk. One may assume ledge and rocky soil and add contingency, while another assumes easy digging. One may include removal and replacement of a deck, and another may leave it out. One may include a new sump pit and the other may drain to daylight. Ask each contractor what assumptions the price depends on and what happens if conditions differ.

Do not choose by the lowest number. Choose by the clearest scope, the best answer on where the water goes and a written plan for the footing, because exterior work that fails costs the most to redo.
