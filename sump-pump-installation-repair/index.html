Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Nashua, NH

A sump pump that stops during a storm leaves a basement to flood. We repair failed pumps, install new ones and add battery backup so the pit keeps working when the power does not.

If the Basement Is Flooding Now

Our 24/7 line is open, so call right away. Do not enter a flooded basement if water is near outlets, the panel or appliances, because water and electricity are a serious hazard. If you can reach the sump pit safely, check that the pump is plugged in and that the float moves freely. If the power is out, a battery backup, if you have one, should be running.

We offer same-day appointments when the schedule allows, and storm weather brings a rush of calls. Describe what the pit looks like and we will tell you what to check while we head your way. If the water is already high, you may also need a water removal service, and we can tell you what to look for.

Why Sump Pumps Fail

Power loss

Storms that raise groundwater also knock out power. Without a backup, the pump stops when it is needed most.

Stuck float switch

The float can jam against the pit wall or a pipe, so the pump never turns on, or never turns off.

Clogged intake

Silt, gravel and debris block the pump inlet and burn out the motor.

Old age

Pumps that run often wear out. Many are replaced after years of service.

Frozen or blocked discharge

In winter, an outside discharge line can freeze solid and the water has nowhere to go.

Undersized pump

A pump that was too small for the inflow runs constantly and fails early.

New Pump, Backup and Alarm

We install submersible and pedestal pumps and size them to the pit and the inflow. A good installation has a check valve to keep discharge from flowing back, a properly routed discharge line, and a pit that is clean and level.

Sump Pumps and Groundwater in Nashua

Nashua sits between the Merrimack and Nashua rivers, with Salmon Brook running through the city. Homes near these waters, and homes in low spots, can see a high water table in spring melt and after heavy rain. A sump pump is common in basements in the area, and many homeowners add a battery backup after one close call.

Hollis and parts of Hudson and Merrimack have private wells, private septic and varied drainage. Do not discharge a sump pump into a septic system. It can overload the leach field and cause a failure. We route discharge to a safe location, and in some towns local rules limit where sump water can go. We ask the town before we connect anywhere.

If water keeps entering because of how the foundation was built, a pump manages the symptom. Our basement waterproofing page explains the options that deal with the cause.

Test Your Pump Before the Storm

1

Check power

Confirm the pump is plugged into a working outlet.

2

Pour water

Pour a few buckets of water into the pit and watch the float rise.

3

Watch it run

The pump should turn on, drain the pit and shut off.

4

Test the backup

Unplug the main pump briefly and see whether the backup runs.

Electrical Safety

Water and power share a basement. The National Electrical Code requires GFCI protection for many basement outlets, and a sump pump should be plugged into a protected, grounded receptacle. Never touch the pump or cord while standing in water.
