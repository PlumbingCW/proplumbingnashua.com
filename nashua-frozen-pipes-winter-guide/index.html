Frozen Pipes in a Nashua, NH Winter: Prevent, Thaw, Repair

If a faucet runs slow or stops in cold weather, a pipe may be frozen. Keep the faucet open, warm the area with a hair dryer and call a plumber if you cannot find the frozen section or see water leaking. If a pipe has already burst, shut off the main water valve first and call. Prevention is simpler than repair: insulate pipes in cold spaces, seal drafts and let faucets drip on the coldest nights.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

Where Pipes Freeze

Exterior walls

Pipes in walls with little insulation, especially under kitchen and bathroom sinks.

Unheated basements and crawlspaces

Lines near rim joists and vents.

Garages and outbuildings

Water lines and hose bibs in unheated space.

Attics

Lines in cold attic space, especially near eaves.

Outdoor faucets

Hoses left connected trap water in the faucet.

Well and pump lines

Pressure tanks and lines in unheated sheds or pits.

Prevent a Freeze

  • ✓Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses before the first hard freeze.
  • ✓Insulate pipes in unheated areas with foam sleeves.
  • ✓Seal drafts near pipes with caulk or spray foam.
  • ✓Open cabinet doors on exterior walls on cold nights.
  • ✓Let a faucet drip, since moving water is harder to freeze.
  • ✓Keep the heat on if you travel.
  • ✓Know where your main shutoff is.

A heating failure is a common trigger. If your boiler stops, read our boiler repair page and call fast.

Thawing a Frozen Pipe Safely

1

Open the faucet

Water needs a way out as the ice melts.

2

Find the freeze

Look for frost on the pipe or feel for a cold spot.

3

Warm gently

Use a hair dryer or a heating pad on the pipe, working from the faucet end toward the freeze.

4

Watch for leaks

Check the pipe as it thaws. A split often shows only when water flows.

Never use an open flame, a torch or a kerosene heater. Do not leave a space heater unattended. If you cannot reach the frozen section, call us.

Local Note: Nashua Winters and Where the Main Valve Is

New Hampshire winters bring weeks of freezing weather, and any house with pipes in exterior walls or unheated cellars is exposed. Nashua water is supplied by Pennichuck Water Works, which can give you information on service line depth and shutoff location for your street. The City of Nashua plumbing code, which adopts the International Plumbing Code, sets minimum burial depths for building sewers, 48 inches in most cases, and that frost-aware thinking applies across the system.

In Hollis, where homes use private wells, pressure tanks and pump lines also need protection. In Hudson, call the water utility if the problem is at the street.

Sources: City of Nashua plumbing code (ecode360), Pennichuck Water Works, NH Employment Security community profiles.

After a Burst

Once the water is off, call your insurer and take photos. Dry the area quickly, because wet drywall and insulation can grow mold. A plumber repairs the pipe, and a drying company handles moisture. If the damage is widespread, ask us about the rest of the line. A burst often shows where the system was weak.

Before You Leave on a Winter Trip

An empty house in January is a risk. If the heat fails while you are away, pipes can freeze and burst with no one there to shut off the water. Before a trip, set the thermostat to a safe temperature rather than turning it off, ask a neighbor to check the house and consider shutting off the main valve and draining the lines if you will be gone for a long time.

  • ✓Leave the heat on at a safe temperature.
  • ✓Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.
  • ✓Know where the main shutoff is and that it turns.
  • ✓Consider a smart thermostat or a freeze sensor that alerts your phone.
  • ✓Have the boiler or furnace serviced in the fall.

Insulation That Actually Helps

Foam pipe sleeves are cheap and work well on pipes in basements and crawlspaces. They slow heat loss but do not stop a freeze if the space gets cold enough for long enough. Seal air leaks at rim joists, around pipe penetrations and at vents. Moving air is the real enemy, so draft sealing often helps more than extra insulation on the pipe.

Heat tape can protect a vulnerable section, but only use products listed for the pipe material and install them exactly as directed. Misused heat tape is a fire risk.

Signs a Pipe Has Already Split

A frozen pipe often gives no sign when it splits. The ice plug holds water back, and you only see a leak after thawing. Watch for a drop in water pressure after a cold snap, wet spots on a ceiling or wall, the sound of running water or a meter that moves when fixtures are off. If you see any of these, shut the main valve, then call.

Our leak detection page explains how we find a split inside a wall without tearing it open.

Rentals, Condos and Shared Walls

In an apartment or condo, a frozen pipe in a shared wall may belong to the building, not to you. Tell your landlord or association at once and keep records of the date, the damage and any water in your unit. Ask who is responsible for the plumbing in the wall and for restoring your finishes. If you rent, check your lease and your renters insurance, which often covers your belongings even when the building owner is responsible for the pipe.

Keep the unit heated and keep cabinet doors open on cold nights, since your habits affect the whole building. A tenant who leaves the heat off can cause damage for the neighbors too.

The Short Version

Insulate, seal drafts, drip faucets on the coldest nights and keep the heat on. If a pipe freezes, open the faucet and warm it gently. If it splits, shut the main valve and call. Know where your main valve is before you need it.
