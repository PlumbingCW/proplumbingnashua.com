Plumber in French Hill, Nashua, NH

French Hill is one of Nashua's best known neighborhoods. It sits just north of downtown, takes its name from the French-Canadian millworkers who settled there, and is known for sloping streets lined with big old trees.

A Historic Part of Nashua

The part of Nashua listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Nashville Historic District lies just north of downtown, centered near the junction of Concord, Amherst and Main streets. It was listed on December 13, 1984, and the area is known today as French Hill. The name comes from a short period in the 1800s when the northern part of the city split off as the separate town of Nashville.

The district's residences were built from the early 1800s into the early 1900s. Its oldest listed house is the Abbot House, dated 1803, and many other buildings date from the 1850s to the 1900s. The styles recorded include Federal, Italianate and Late Victorian.

Living and Working in Older Buildings

A Nashua Telegraph neighborhood profile notes that French Hill has many apartments, so you may be a homeowner, a tenant or a landlord. Whichever you are, the useful first step is the same: tell us the address, the number of units and what the water is doing.

Plumbing in a building of this age may have been changed many times, so we check what is actually installed before we recommend work. If your property is in a historic district, ask the City of Nashua planning office whether any review applies before you change anything on the outside, such as a new vent for a water heater.
