Detecting Water Leaks in Your House in Nashua, NH

The fastest way to detect a water leak in a house is a meter test. Turn off every fixture, read your water meter, wait 30 minutes with no water use and read it again. If the reading changed, water is flowing somewhere. Then check toilets with a drop of food coloring in the tank, inspect under sinks and around the water heater and look for stains on ceilings and walls.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

Step One: The Meter Test

1

Shut everything off

Close taps, stop the washer and dishwasher and make sure no one flushes.

2

Read the meter

Note the numbers, or watch the small leak indicator dial.

3

Wait

Leave everything off for 30 minutes to an hour.

4

Read again

Any change means water is flowing.

If the meter does not move, a leak is not running right now. A slow drip that stops and starts, or one that shows only under pressure, may need a longer test or a pressure gauge.

Step Two: Check the Usual Suspects

Toilets

Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank. If color shows in the bowl after 15 minutes without flushing, the flapper leaks.

Under sinks

Feel supply lines and drain connections for dampness.

Water heater

Look at the base, the relief valve pipe and connections.

Washing machine

Check hoses and the shutoff valves.

Outdoor faucets

Look for drips and wet soil at the foundation.

Irrigation

Look for wet patches and low pressure zones.

Step Three: Look for Hidden Signs

  • ✓Stains or bubbling paint on ceilings and walls.
  • ✓A musty smell in a closet or basement.
  • ✓Warm spots on a floor.
  • ✓Hissing or trickling sounds in walls.
  • ✓A wet patch in the yard between the meter and the house.
  • ✓Unexplained mold or mildew.

Water travels. A stain on a ceiling may sit far from the leak. Our leak detection page explains how we trace it.

Local Note: Nashua Water, Meters and Billing

Pennichuck Water Works supplies most of Nashua and can answer billing questions. Many homes have the meter in the basement near where the line enters. A water meter reading and a leak indicator on the dial are standard tools for this test. If you have a high bill and the meter does not move, the cause may be billing timing or seasonal use, and Pennichuck can explain the statement.

In Hudson, the town water utility handles billing. In Hollis, homes use private wells and there is no water bill, so the clue is a pump that runs constantly or a pressure tank that cycles often.

Sources: Pennichuck Water Works, Hudson Water Utility, NH Employment Security community profiles.

When to Call a Plumber

Call if the meter moves and you cannot find the cause, if you see water coming through a ceiling or wall, if a stain keeps growing or if you hear water in a wall. A leak you cannot see is the kind that does the most damage. We find it with acoustic and thermal tools, and we confirm before we open anything. If a pipe has split, our burst pipe repair page lists emergency steps.

Keep Leaks From Returning

Replace old supply lines and rubber hoses. Know where your main shutoff is. Test it once a year and make sure it turns. Install leak alarms near the water heater, the washing machine and under sinks. Check the meter again after repairs to confirm the problem is gone.

Tools That Help at Home

  • ✓A flashlight and a dry paper towel for checking under sinks and around fixtures.
  • ✓Food coloring for the toilet dye test.
  • ✓A water leak alarm that sits on the floor and sounds when it gets wet.
  • ✓A moisture meter, which shows damp materials behind a wall surface.
  • ✓A smart water monitor, which fits on the main line and tracks flow.

None of these replaces a plumber, but they help you narrow the problem. A leak alarm near the water heater, under the kitchen sink and beside the washing machine is a cheap upgrade that catches the most common failures early.

Reading Your Water Bill

Your water bill is a leak detector too. Compare the last several months, ideally the same season in prior years. A steady rise with no change in household use is a warning. A single high month may be a one-time event such as filling a pool or an irrigation test. A bill that is high in winter, when you are not watering a lawn, is more suspicious than one that is high in summer.

If the bill and the meter disagree, ask the utility for a meter reading history and whether the meter was recently replaced. Pennichuck can explain billing. We find where the water goes on your side of the meter.

What We Do When You Call

We ask when you noticed the problem, what changed and where you see signs. We run the meter test, isolate zones of the house and use acoustic and thermal tools to pinpoint the leak. We confirm the location and explain the repair before cutting. If the leak is outside, between the meter and the house, our water service line page covers the next step.

Leaks That Come and Go

Some leaks only run under certain conditions. A washing machine valve may drip only while the machine fills. A toilet may leak only when the tank is full. A pipe joint may seep only when the water heater cycles and the pipe expands. If your first meter test shows nothing, repeat it at a different time, and run each fixture on its own while you watch the meter. Note when the signs appear and tell us, because the timing is often the clue that points to the source.

The Short Version

Start with the meter, check toilets, sinks and the water heater, and look for stains, smells and sounds. If the meter moves and you cannot find the source, call. A leak found early is a small repair, and a leak found late is a remodel.
