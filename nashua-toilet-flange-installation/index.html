Installing a New Toilet Flange in Nashua, NH Homes

A toilet flange is the ring on the floor that anchors the toilet and connects it to the drain. If your toilet rocks, leaks at the base or the flange is cracked, rusted or sitting below the finished floor, it needs repair or replacement. A new flange is set on a solid floor, tied to the drain pipe and secured with bolts. The flange should sit on top of the finished floor, not below it, so the toilet seals against it.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published October 16, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

What the Flange Does

The toilet flange does three jobs. It holds the toilet to the floor with two closet bolts. It connects the toilet to the drain pipe. And it provides a flat surface for the wax or foam seal that keeps water and sewer gas inside the pipe. When the flange fails, the seal fails and the toilet leaks.

Flanges come in several materials. Plastic flanges are common in newer homes. Cast iron and brass flanges are common in older ones. Cast iron can rust through at the bolt slots, and a rusted flange cannot hold bolts.

Signs You Need a New Flange

Toilet rocks

Loose bolts or a broken flange. Tightening bolts on a broken flange does not fix it.

Water at the base

A failed seal, often from a flange that sits too low or has moved.

Cracked or rusted ring

You can see this once the toilet is lifted. A broken slot cannot hold a bolt.

Flange below the floor

New flooring was laid over the old, and the flange now sits below it.

Sewer smell

A seal that no longer closes lets odor into the room.

Soft floor

A long leak softens the subfloor, which then needs repair before the flange is set.

How a Flange Is Replaced

1

Remove the toilet

We shut off the water, drain the tank, lift the toilet and clean off the old seal.

2

Check the floor

We probe for soft wood. Rot gets repaired before anything else.

3

Set the flange

A repair ring, a new flange or a flange extender is fastened to solid subfloor and connected to the pipe.

4

Reset the toilet

The toilet goes on a new seal with new bolts and is leveled and tested.

The flange height matters. It should sit flush with, or slightly above, the finished floor. A flange that is too low needs an extender or a spacer. A flange that is too high can prevent the toilet from seating correctly.

Local Note: Older Nashua Floors and Permits

Older Nashua homes, such as those near Downtown and the Tree Streets, may have had plumbing changed many times, so check what your toilet actually sits on. Replacing flooring around the toilet is a common reason for a flange problem, because the flange ends up below the new floor. The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code with local amendments, and the Department of Building Safety can tell you whether a flange repair needs a permit.

We repair toilets in Hudson and Merrimack as well. In Hudson, a New Hampshire licensed plumber is required to pull a plumbing permit, and the Fire Department Inspectional Services Division handles inspections.

Do Not Skip the Floor Check

A toilet that has leaked for months can rot the subfloor. If we find soft wood, we say so. Setting a new flange on rotten wood only starts the problem again. Floor repair can be as simple as a patch of plywood, or as big as a section of subfloor. Tell your contractor before the flooring goes back.

Choosing a Flange Repair Method

The right repair depends on how the flange failed. A cracked plastic flange that is still glued to a sound pipe can be cut out and replaced. A flange that is rusted on cast iron may take a repair ring that bolts over the old one. A flange with a damaged pipe underneath may need a longer repair that opens the floor.

Repair ring

A metal or plastic ring that fastens to the floor and gives new bolt slots when the old flange is cracked at the edge but the pipe is sound.

Replacement flange

A new flange glued or clamped to the pipe. It needs the pipe to be in good condition.

Extender

A spacer that lifts a flange that sits below the finished floor.

Closet bend repair

When the pipe itself is damaged, the repair moves down into the floor and takes more work.

We choose the least invasive method that will last, and we tell you if the floor needs attention first.

Wax Ring or Wax-Free Seal

The seal between the toilet and the flange is wax or a wax-free foam or rubber gasket. Both work when the flange is level and at the right height. A wax ring is cheap and familiar, but it is a one-time seal. If you lift a toilet, replace the ring. A wax-free seal is forgiving of small height differences. Either way, do not reuse an old seal, and do not stack two rings to compensate for a low flange. That is what an extender is for.

Signs You Need a Plumber Rather Than a Weekend Fix

A repair ring and a new seal are within reach for many homeowners, but some situations call for a plumber. A flange on cast iron or lead pipe, a flange with a cracked pipe beneath it, a soft or stained floor, or a toilet that leaks sewage and not just clean tank water all need careful work. A mistake can leave sewer gas in the room or a slow leak under the floor. Call us if you are unsure.
