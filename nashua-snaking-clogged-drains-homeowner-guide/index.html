Snaking Clogged Tubs, Showers and Floor Drains in Nashua, NH

Tubs, showers and floor drains clog for different reasons than kitchen sinks, and they need a different snake approach. Tubs and showers clog with hair and soap, so a short hand snake or a drain claw usually works. Floor drains collect sediment and grease and often connect to the main, so a clog there deserves a careful look. If more than one fixture is slow, skip the snake and call.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published October 30, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

Match the Tool to the Drain

Drain type, usual clog and first tool
DrainUsual clogFirst tool
TubHair and soap in the overflow or trapDrain claw or short snake
ShowerHair mat near the strainerDrain claw, then a snake
Bathroom sinkHair and toothpaste film in the stopper areaPull the stopper, clean the trap
Basement floor drainSilt, lint and greaseHand snake through the grate opening
Main cleanoutRoots, wipes, greaseMachine, by a plumber

The right tool is the smallest one that works. A big machine in a small drain can scratch the pipe and a small snake in a big pipe just makes a hole.

Snaking a Tub or Shower

1

Remove the stopper or strainer

Unscrew the strainer or lift the stopper and clear what you can see.

2

Pull the hair

A plastic drain claw hooks hair better than any chemical.

3

Snake if needed

Feed a short hand snake through the drain and turn it as you push.

4

Flush

Run hot water for a minute and watch the flow.

If the tub drains but gurgles in the nearby toilet, the clog may be in the shared branch. Stop and call, because snaking the tub will not clear it.

Floor Drains and Main Cleanouts

A basement floor drain is the lowest opening in the house, so it is the first place sewage appears in a main line backup. If the drain overflows when you run the washer or flush a toilet upstairs, the main is blocked. Do not snake it from the floor drain and do not use chemicals. Call us, because a backup is an emergency and the right access point is the main cleanout.

The cleanout is a capped pipe, usually near where the main leaves the house. Opening it can release water, so do not remove the cap if the line is full. Our sewer line cleaning page explains how we handle it.

Nashua was incorporated as a town in 1746 and became a city in 1853, and its mill-era neighborhoods date from the nineteenth century. When to Stop Snaking

  • ✓The snake stops hard and will not advance.
  • ✓You pull back black sludge and the drain does not improve.
  • ✓Water in a second fixture changes when you run the first.
  • ✓The drain clears and returns in days.
  • ✓You smell sewage in the basement.

At any of those, a camera look at the pipe makes more sense than another pass with the snake. See our sewer inspection page.

Prevent the Next Clog

A hair catcher costs little and saves a lot of trouble. Pull and clean it every week or two. Keep wipes, floss, cotton swabs and feminine products out of the toilet. Run hot water after heavy use of the kitchen sink. If you have a floor drain you never use, pour a bit of water into the trap now and then so the water seal does not dry out and let sewer gas in.

Hair, Soap and Why Tubs Clog

Hair does not dissolve. In a tub or shower it binds with soap scum into a plug that sits just below the strainer or in the trap. Over weeks, the plug grows until water cannot pass. This is why a drain claw often works better than a snake, because it pulls the plug out instead of pushing it farther down.

Long hair wraps around a snake cable and can jam the cable in the trap. If your snake comes up with a mass of hair, you cleared the clog, but check that no strands remain in the trap arm. Clean the stopper as well, since the pivot rod under a pop-up stopper catches hair and slows the drain.

Reading What the Snake Tells You

Soft resistance, then free

You broke through a soft clog. Flush with hot water and check the flow.

Hard stop at a fixed distance

You hit a fitting, a damaged pipe or a solid object. Do not force it.

Cable comes back with roots

Roots are in the line. A repair, not just a cleaning, is the long-term answer.

Cable comes back clean

The clog may be past the cable reach or in a different branch.

If the snake tells you the problem is a root or a damaged pipe, move on to the camera. Our sewer inspection page explains what we see and what we recommend.

Safety When You Work With a Snake

Wear gloves and eye protection, and keep the cable controlled when you crank it. A snake can whip if it is released under tension. Never use a power auger in a fixture unless you have experience, since the cable can twist and injure your hand. If you are working near an open cleanout, keep your face back, because pressure or wastewater can surge out when the clog breaks free.
