Repiping in Nashua, NH

Replacing worn out supply lines to stop leaks, restore pressure and clear brown water. We replace whole houses or single runs with PEX or copper and plan around your walls and your schedule.

Signs Your Supply Lines Are Failing

Repeated pinhole leaks

One pinhole leak is a repair. A second or third in a few years often means the whole run is thinning.

Low water pressure

Corrosion narrows old steel pipe from the inside until little flows through.

Brown or rust colored water

Galvanized steel and some old fittings shed rust, especially after the water sits.

Stains and moisture

Stains on ceilings and walls, or damp spots in a basement, point to a leak in a hidden line.

Visible corrosion

Green or white crust on copper joints, or flaking on steel pipe, signals aging.

Plumbing from a known risky era

Galvanized steel, polybutylene and some early plastic or copper joints fail with age.

What Repiping Involves

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry water to your fixtures. We plan routes through walls, floors and basement ceilings to keep openings small. For many homes, new lines run from a central manifold in the basement or utility room, with a line for each fixture group.

1

Inspect and plan

We trace the existing lines and plan new routes with you.

2

Protect the house

We protect floors and furniture and shut off water for a planned time.

3

Replace the lines

New lines are run, connected and pressure tested before walls close.

4

Patch access points

We repair or leave small openings ready for a drywall finisher.

If you only need part of the system replaced, we say so. A single failing run does not always justify repiping the whole house.

PEX or Copper

PEX and copper supply lines compared
QuestionPEXCopper
InstallationFlexible, fewer joints, faster in finished wallsRigid, soldered joints, more labor
Freeze toleranceHandles expansion better than rigid pipe, but is not freeze proofCan split when frozen
CorrosionDoes not corrode like metalCan corrode with some water chemistry
Best fitMost whole-house repipesExposed runs, where code or preference calls for metal

We recommend what suits your house and explain the reasons. Neither material replaces good installation, and neither is a fix for a freeze if pipes are left in an unheated space without protection.

Repiping Older Nashua Homes

Nashua has many older homes, including in neighborhoods such as Downtown, French Hill and the Tree Streets. Some homes of that age may still have galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside, and most have a mix of repairs added over decades, so we confirm what you have before quoting. A repipe can solve pressure, color and leak problems when the pipes really are the cause.

Pennichuck reports that water leaving its plants contains no lead or copper, and that any lead risk comes from the service lines and plumbing in the home. If you are curious what your service line is made of, see our water service line page, and Pennichuck publishes a service line inventory. We replace lead or galvanized pipe inside the house as part of repiping.

Planning Around Permits and Inspection

Repiping is usually permitted work. We ask the City of Nashua Department of Building Safety, or the local office in Hudson, Merrimack or Hollis, what applies. In Hudson, a New Hampshire licensed plumber is required to pull the permit and inspections are scheduled with the town. We handle the paperwork with you.

Codes and Permits

Repiping is governed by the plumbing code your town has adopted, usually based on the International Plumbing Code or Uniform Plumbing Code. The building office confirms whether a permit and inspection are required for your address, and we will schedule around that.
