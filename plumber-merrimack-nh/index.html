What to Know About Water in Merrimack

Merrimack has more than one water supplier, and the supplier matters when you call.

Two municipal suppliers

Parts of town are served by Pennichuck Water Works and parts by the Merrimack Village District, which draws on six groundwater wells and runs an iron and manganese treatment facility. Ask the district if you are not sure which supplies your street.

PFAS and alternate water

NHDES found PFOA in a district tap sample in 2016 and placed Merrimack inside the Saint-Gobain consent decree outer boundary. Alternate water has been provided to hundreds of connections, and the district posts sampling results. If you are on a private well, ask NHDES about testing.

Municipal sewer

The town runs its own wastewater plant, so most backups are on your side of the street connection. We camera the line to show where the trouble sits.

Home water treatment

Many Merrimack homes have point-of-entry filters. We shut them off properly when we replace a water heater or a supply line, and restore them after.

Before We Arrive: Active Leak or Burst Pipe

Do these while we drive over. They take a few minutes and they limit what the water reaches.

Close the main

Find the main valve, often near the meter or the point where water enters, and turn it clockwise. If a filter system sits near it, leave the bypass alone.

Cut power where it is wet

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground.

Move what you can

Lift furniture legs onto blocks and get paper and electronics off the floor.

Merrimack Roads and History

Merrimack was incorporated in 1746. It sits on US Route 3, NH 101A and the Everett Turnpike, with Exit 11 about two miles from the center. Thornton's Ferry was named for Matthew Thornton, a signer of the Declaration of Independence. Old river villages have older plumbing than the hillside developments, and the difference shows in what we find under sinks and in basements.
