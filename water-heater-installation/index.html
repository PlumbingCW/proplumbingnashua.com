Water Heater Installation in Nashua, NH

Planning a new water heater, a first installation in an addition or an upgrade in fuel type or size. We plan the install around your household, your flue and your gas or electric service.

Planning an Installation Before Something Breaks

Most installations come after a tank fails. A planned installation is better, because you choose the unit, check the venting and gas supply and schedule the work at a convenient time. If you are weighing a new heater for a renovation, an addition or an aging tank that is still working, this page is for you. If your tank has already failed, go to our water heater replacement page for same-day help.

We start with the household. How many people use hot water, how many bathrooms, whether anyone runs a dishwasher and a shower at the same time, and whether you expect the household to change. Then we look at the room: where the unit sits, how it vents, what fuel is available and how much clearance there is.

Sizing and Fuel Choices

Water heater types compared
TypeGood fitWatch for
Gas tankHomes with gas service and a safe flueVenting and combustion air need to be right
Electric tankHomes without gas or a flueHigher running cost in many homes
Hybrid heat pumpBasements and larger rooms with airflowNeeds space, condensate drain and cool air around it
Tankless gasHouseholds that want on-demand hot waterGas line size, venting and descaling

We recommend based on what your house can support, not what is easiest to install. A hybrid unit in a small closet will perform poorly. A tankless unit on an undersized gas line will not keep up. We tell you that before you buy.

Installation Details That Matter

  • ✓Venting: a gas unit needs a flue sized and pitched for the appliance, with proper clearance from combustibles.
  • ✓Gas supply: the gas line must carry enough volume for the appliance, and joints are leak tested.
  • ✓Relief valve and discharge: the temperature and pressure relief valve needs a properly routed discharge pipe.
  • ✓Expansion control: a closed system often needs an expansion tank to keep pressure in range.
  • ✓Shutoffs and drain: an accessible shutoff valve and drain make future service easier.

The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code with local amendments covering water heater temperature settings and tempering. For gas work, New Hampshire licenses plumbers and gas fitters through the Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board. We ask the Department of Building Safety whether your installation needs a permit, and we recommend you verify any contractor you hire holds the right license.

Where We Install in the Nashua Area

We install water heaters throughout Nashua, including older homes near Downtown and the Tree Streets and newer homes in South Nashua and off Amherst Street. Space, venting and access vary from house to house, so we look at the basement or utility room before we recommend a unit. We also work in Hudson, Merrimack and Hollis.

Hollis homes rely on private wells, so we check water quality and pressure details with you before sizing. Hudson and Merrimack homes may sit on municipal systems with different water chemistry, and we account for that when we recommend materials and maintenance.

Maintenance After Installation

A new heater lasts longer when it is maintained. We show you the shutoffs, the temperature setting and the relief valve test. Annual flushing and anode checks are cheap relative to a premature tank failure, and we tell you what schedule makes sense for your water.
