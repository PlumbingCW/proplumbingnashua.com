Safety First When a Disposal Fails

Our 24/7 line is open. Turn the disposal off at the wall switch before you do anything else, and never put your hand into the grinding chamber. If water is leaking underneath, place a bucket under the unit, switch off the power at the breaker and call. A leak at the disposal can run into the cabinet floor and cause damage that is harder to repair than the disposal.

We offer same-day appointments when the schedule allows, because a dead disposal can back up the sink and stop meal prep. Tell us the brand on the label if you can see it.

What Goes Wrong With Disposals

Humming but not turning

A jam. Often a hard object or fibrous scraps lodged between the flywheel and the shredder ring. We clear it from below, not by hand.

Silent, no hum

A tripped reset button, a failed switch, a dead outlet or a burned motor.

Leaks at the top

A failed sink flange seal or mounting gasket.

Leaks at the side or bottom

A cracked housing, a failed drain connection or a worn internal seal. A cracked housing means replacement.

Slow draining sink

A clog past the disposal, or a partly blocked dishwasher connection.

Bad smell

Food residue on the walls and baffle. A cleaning often helps.

Repair or Replacement

A jam or a reset is a quick fix. A bad switch or a loose connection is repairable. A cracked housing, a failed motor or a leaking body means a new disposal. If a unit is old and needs repairs, replacement often makes more sense than another fix.

We install new disposals with a fresh mounting assembly, check the dishwasher connection and the drain trap, and test the unit before we leave. If the sink has a slow drain unrelated to the disposal, we clear it. A disposal that backs up with every use may point to a clogged kitchen line, and our drain cleaning page explains how we handle that.

What Should and Should Not Go Down a Disposal

Fine in small amounts

  • Soft food scraps
  • Small pieces of cooked vegetables
  • Ice cubes, occasionally
  • Citrus peels in small bits

Keep out of the disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts
  • Potato peels, pasta and rice in volume
  • Bones, pits and fibrous stalks

Grease is the biggest problem. It cools in the line and builds up, even if the disposal grinds it. Run cold water while you use the unit and for a short time afterward.

Disposals in Nashua Homes

Many Nashua kitchens are on municipal sewer, where a disposal adds to the load on the building line. A disposal can expose a slow kitchen line quickly in any home, so we check the line first. In Hollis and other septic areas, a disposal adds solids to the tank and can shorten the interval between pumpings. We talk through that before installing one.
