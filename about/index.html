About Nashua Pro Plumbing

Nashua Pro Plumbing is a Nashua-based plumbing service for homes and businesses in Nashua, Hudson, Merrimack and Hollis, New Hampshire.

10 Technology Way, Nashua24/7 emergency linePlain-language plumbing advice

Who We Are

We are based at 10 Technology Way in Nashua, New Hampshire. Our phone line is open around the clock, and plumbers are dispatched from Nashua to homes and businesses in the surrounding area.

We keep our service area small on purpose. Knowing the water, the sewer and the permit rules in each town lets us give honest answers on the first call.

How We Work

We ask questions, tell you what to shut off and give an arrival window. On site, we find the cause, explain your options and give a clear price before work starts. We do not push work you do not need, and we tell you when a job belongs to the city, the water utility or another trade.

For work that needs a permit, we ask the local building office what applies to your address. For gas work and licensing, we point you to the New Hampshire Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board so you can verify any contractor.

What We Handle

Drains and sewers, water heaters, leaks, burst pipes, toilets and faucets, sump pumps, boilers and commercial plumbing. Browse the full list on our services page, or read about the towns we serve on our service area page.

What a Typical Call Looks Like

Most calls fall into a handful of patterns. A kitchen drain that will not move. A water heater that has stopped heating or started leaking. A toilet that runs or overflows. A pipe that froze overnight and split. A basement with an inch of water and a sump pump that has quit. For each, we ask the same questions in the same order, because the answers decide what you should shut off and how urgent the visit is.

If the problem is a gas smell, we tell you to leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. If it is a sewage backup, we ask you to keep people away from the wet area. If it is a flooded basement with live electrical equipment, we ask you to stay out until the power is off. Safety instructions come before scheduling.

Local Knowledge That Shapes the Work

Nashua draws most of its water from the Pennichuck Water Works Nashua system, which treats water from the Merrimack River and Pennichuck Brook and reports soft water. The city runs a municipal sewer and wastewater plant. Hudson has its own water utility, Merrimack is served by Pennichuck and the Merrimack Village District, and Hollis relies on private wells and septic systems. Those differences change which parts we bring, which questions we ask and who you call when a problem sits outside your property line.

The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code with local amendments, and the Department of Building Safety answers permit questions. Hudson routes plumbing permits through its Fire Department Inspectional Services Division. We ask the right office before work that needs a permit, so you hear it from the source.

How to Check Any Plumber You Hire

New Hampshire licenses plumbers and gas fitters through the Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board, which sits under the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Ask any contractor, including us, for license details, and verify them with the board. Ask for a clear price before work starts. Ask what happens if the job turns out different from what was expected. A good plumber answers all three without hesitation.

You can also ask about insurance, about who will be on site and about how the company handles a problem after the visit. Our line is open 24/7, and you can call about a past job too.

What We Promise and What We Do Not

We promise to answer the phone, to explain what we find in plain words and to give a clear price before work starts. We promise to tell you when a repair is not worth doing, when a job belongs to the city or a utility, and when it needs another trade such as an electrician, a well specialist or a gas utility.

We do not promise arrival times we cannot keep, and we do not promise results that depend on conditions we have not seen. If a pipe is hidden in a wall, we tell you what we think and what we will confirm once we look. You should hear the truth from us before you hear a surprise from the invoice.

If you ever have a question about a past visit, call the same line and ask. We would rather hear about a problem than lose your trust over it.
