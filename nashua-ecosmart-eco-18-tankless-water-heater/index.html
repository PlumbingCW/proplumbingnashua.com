EcoSmart ECO 18 Tankless Water Heater: A Nashua, NH Guide

The EcoSmart ECO 18 is an electric tankless water heater, which means it heats water with electric elements as you use it. Whether it works in a Nashua house depends mostly on your electrical panel and how cold the incoming water is in winter. A large electric tankless unit needs a big dedicated circuit, and cold New Hampshire groundwater limits how much hot water it can deliver at once.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published November 6, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

What an Electric Tankless Unit Needs

Electric tankless units trade a tank for power. They need a lot of electricity at once, so the house must have spare capacity in the panel and a dedicated circuit with the right breaker and wire size. Check the manufacturer spec sheet for the exact load. If the panel is full or the service is small, you may need an electrician and a service upgrade.

  • ✓Panel capacity for the unit load on top of the rest of the house.
  • ✓A dedicated circuit with the breaker and wire size the manufacturer lists.
  • ✓A place to mount the unit near the plumbing, within the clearances on the label.
  • ✓Water lines that carry enough flow.
  • ✓An electrician to do the electrical work.

Cold Groundwater and Flow Limits

Every tankless heater raises the temperature of incoming water by a certain number of degrees at a given flow. When incoming water is cold, as it is in a New Hampshire winter, the unit has to do more, and the flow it can serve at your set temperature falls. A unit that serves two showers in August may struggle with one and a dishwasher in January.

Electric Tankless or Gas Tankless

Neither is better for everyone. Our tankless water heater page explains how we choose.

Electric tankless

  • No venting, easier placement
  • Needs a large electrical circuit
  • Often used at one point of use or in smaller households

Gas tankless

  • Higher flow in cold weather
  • Needs gas line sizing and venting
  • Usually serves a whole house

Local Note: Nashua Water and Code

Pennichuck reports core hardness near 21 ppm for Nashua, which is soft, so scale builds more slowly here than in much of the country. The City of Nashua plumbing code caps faucet and tankless delivered water temperature and calls for tempering protection, so the unit must be set to meet it. Ask the Department of Building Safety whether the electrical and plumbing work need permits.

In Hudson and Merrimack, water chemistry differs by supplier, so flush schedules should match your water.

Sources: Pennichuck Water Works Consumer Confidence Report, City of Nashua plumbing code (ecode360).

Questions to Ask Before You Buy

  • ✓What is the unit load, and does my panel handle it?
  • ✓What flow will it deliver in January water temperature?
  • ✓Where will it mount, and does it meet clearances?
  • ✓Who will do the electrical work?
  • ✓How will it be flushed and descaled?

If your gas service is large and your electric panel is small, gas may be easier. Compare with a good gas tank as well, because a tank is often the least troublesome choice.

Where Electric Tankless Makes Sense

Small point-of-use units can work for a remote bathroom or a garage sink. A whole-house electric tankless can work in a house with a strong electrical service and moderate hot water use. For a large family in an older house with a small panel, the electrical upgrade can outweigh the benefit. We help you decide.

Running the Numbers for Your House

To size a tankless unit, add up the flow of the fixtures you expect to run at once. A showerhead, a kitchen faucet and a dishwasher each draw a different flow. Then look at the temperature rise needed, which is the difference between the incoming water temperature and the temperature you want. In winter, groundwater in New Hampshire is cold, so the rise is large. The manufacturer publishes a chart that shows how much flow the unit can heat at a given rise.

If the chart shows the unit cannot serve your peak demand in winter, you have options. Use two smaller units for separate zones, shave peak demand by staggering showers and laundry, or choose a gas unit or a tank. Our water heater installation page explains those choices.

Maintenance for Electric Units

Electric tankless units have no flue and no burner, so there is less to service. They still develop scale on the heating elements in hard water, they have an inlet screen that clogs and they have flow sensors that can fail. Flush the unit on a schedule that fits your water, clean the screen and have the connections checked. A unit that is serviced runs longer and heats more reliably.

When a Tank Is the Better Answer

If your electrical panel is nearly full, if your household runs several showers at once or if you want the simplest installation, a standard tank may serve you better than an electric tankless unit. A tank stores hot water, so it can supply a burst of demand without a huge electrical load. A hybrid heat pump tank uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Compare the options for your house before you spend on panel upgrades, and ask for the total installed scope, not just the price of the unit.

The Short Version

An electric tankless unit suits a house with strong electrical service, moderate hot water use and a good reason to avoid venting. It is a poor fit for a large family on a small panel. Check the manufacturer sizing chart against January water temperatures, price the electrical work with the unit, and compare against a tank before you commit.
