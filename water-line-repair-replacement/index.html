Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Nashua, NH

The water service line runs from the street to your house, and when it fails you may lose pressure, see a wet yard or get a surprising bill. We find the problem and repair or replace the line.

If Your Service Line Has Failed

Our 24/7 line is open, so call if water is pouring into a yard, pressure has fallen or the meter is spinning with all fixtures off. Close the main valve inside the house. If the leak is between the street and the house, closing it should stop water inside the home, but the leak outside may continue until the supply is shut off at the curb or the meter. Tell us what you see and we will explain who shuts off what.

We offer same-day appointments when the schedule allows. A broken service line is urgent because it wastes water and can wash out the soil and undermine a walkway or driveway.

Signs of a Service Line Problem

Wet or soft spot in the yard

Water coming up between the meter and the house, even in dry weather.

Sudden low pressure

A break or a heavily corroded section limits flow to the house.

Meter moves with fixtures off

Water is flowing somewhere past the meter. We use a test to find where.

Brown or cloudy water

Old steel or a failing section can shed rust and soil.

High water bill

A hidden leak in the line can run for weeks without a visible sign.

Sinkhole or sunken ground

Soil washes out around the break and the ground sinks.

Repair and Replacement Options

A short break can be repaired with a section of new pipe and fittings. A line that has corroded throughout or been repaired many times is usually better replaced. We explain what we find and what each option involves, including how much digging is needed.

Who Owns What in Nashua

In Nashua, Pennichuck Water Works supplies water. The utility owns and maintains its main and equipment in the street. The service line from the main or curb stop to your house is generally the property owner responsibility, but the exact split depends on the utility rules and where your line connects. Ask Pennichuck where your responsibility starts and ends before any digging, and we work with you on the private side.

Pennichuck reports that water leaving its plants has no lead or copper, and that lead risk comes from service lines and plumbing in homes. The utility publishes a service line inventory project. If your line is lead or galvanized, replacement is worth planning, and we can do the private side of it.

Service Lines Across Nashua, Hudson, Merrimack and Hollis

Older homes in Downtown, French Hill and the Tree Streets may have a century of changes in the line, with old steel, copper and plastic sections joined over time. Newer homes off Amherst Street and in South Nashua often have copper or plastic service lines that last well. Frost matters in New Hampshire, because a shallow line can freeze in a hard winter.

In Hudson, the town water utility serves many homes and has completed a lead service line inventory. In Merrimack, water comes from Pennichuck or the Merrimack Village District depending on the address. In Hollis, homes use private wells, so the line runs from the well and pressure tank, and the repair path is different.
