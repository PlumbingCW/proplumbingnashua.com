Boiler Circulator Pump Repair for Nashua, NH Homes

A boiler circulator pump pushes hot water through your heating loop. When it fails, the boiler fires but the radiators stay cold. Common signs are a humming motor that does not move water, a grinding noise, a pump that is hot to the touch with no flow and zones that heat unevenly. Many circulator pumps are replaced as a unit and the job takes a couple of hours, so a no-heat call in winter is a repair we move to the front.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

What the Circulator Does

A hydronic heating system has a boiler that heats water and a network of pipes that carries it to radiators or baseboard. The circulator is a small pump on the supply or return line that keeps the water moving. Without it, the boiler heats a tank of water that nobody sees.

Some homes have one circulator and zone valves. Others have a circulator for each zone. Knowing which you have helps us find the failed one, and we start by feeling which pipes are hot and which are cold.

Signs the Pump Is Failing

Boiler hot, radiators cold

The boiler is making heat but the water is not leaving.

Humming without flow

The motor runs but the shaft or impeller has seized or failed.

Grinding or rattling

Worn bearings or debris in the housing.

Leak at the flange

A weeping gasket or a failing seal at the pump flange.

One zone cold

A zone pump or valve has failed, while the other zones work.

Pump casing very hot

The pump is running without moving water.

Repair or Replacement

Most circulators are sealed units. If the motor fails, the whole unit is replaced, but the flanges and isolation valves stay. Wet-rotor pumps, common in homes, are replaced as a cartridge or a full pump depending on the model. We match the flow rating and the head, not just the flange pattern.

1

Isolate and drain

We close the isolation valves and relieve pressure so only the pump section loses water.

2

Swap the pump

The old pump comes out and the new one goes in with fresh gaskets.

3

Bleed the loop

We purge air so the pump moves water rather than air.

4

Test the zones

We confirm each zone heats and check pressure.

If the system has no isolation valves, we may need to drain part of the loop. A good installation adds isolation valves so the next change is cleaner.

Local Note: Winter, Wiring and Licensing in Nashua

A house that loses heat during a New Hampshire cold snap can see pipes freeze, so a failed pump is more than a comfort issue. If you suspect a frozen line, our burst pipe repair page lists the first steps.

In New Hampshire, plumbers and gas fitters are licensed through the Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board under the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification. Ask any contractor for license details. The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code with local amendments, and the Department of Building Safety is the place to ask about permits for boiler replacement.

Why Pumps Fail

  • ✓Age and hours of use. Pumps run all winter.
  • ✓Air in the system, which can starve the bearings.
  • ✓Sludge and magnetite from old iron piping.
  • ✓Electrical issues in the motor or control.
  • ✓Running dry after a leak drains the loop.

If a pump fails early, we ask why. A system with lots of sludge or air problems will kill the next pump too. A flush or an air eliminator may be part of the fix.

Keeping the System Healthy

Have the boiler serviced before the heating season. Check the pressure gauge now and then. Bleed radiators that are cold at the top. If you have an older steam or hot water system with iron radiators, ask about a system flush. We also serve Hudson and Merrimack.

What to Check Before You Call

A few quick checks help us diagnose by phone. Look at the thermostat and confirm it is calling for heat. Look at the boiler for a lit display or an error code. Check the pressure gauge, which should sit within the range printed on the label, and feel the pipes on either side of the pump. If the supply pipe is hot and the return is cold, water is not moving.

  • ✓Is the circulator humming, silent or noisy?
  • ✓Is the pump housing hot to the touch?
  • ✓Is there water at the pump flanges?
  • ✓Do all zones fail or only one?
  • ✓Did the problem start after a power outage or a repair?

Do not open bleed valves on a hot system without a hose and a bucket, and do not try to run the boiler with the pump off. A boiler that cannot shed heat can trip its high limit and shut down, which protects the boiler and also leaves you without heat.

Pump Upgrades Worth Considering

When we replace a pump, we sometimes recommend a different model. A properly sized variable speed circulator uses less electricity than an old fixed speed pump and can lower noise, but it must suit the system. A pump that is too strong can make flow noise in baseboard, and one that is too weak leaves far rooms cold. We match the pump to the loop and tell you why.

Pump Noise in Cold Snaps

Circulators work hardest during cold snaps, which is when a worn bearing finally gives out. If you hear a new hum or rattle in December, call before the weather turns, because a failed pump at the coldest hour means a rush call and a cold house. Early replacement is calmer and cheaper.
