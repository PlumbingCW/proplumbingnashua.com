How to Snake a Kitchen Sink Drain in Nashua, NH

A kitchen sink drain snake is a flexible steel cable that you feed into the pipe to break up or hook a clog. For a single slow kitchen sink, a hand-crank snake of about 25 feet, run through the trap or the wall opening, clears most clogs in about twenty minutes. If the water backs up in other fixtures or the snake stops at a long distance, the problem is in the main line and you should call a plumber.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published October 6, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

What a Drain Snake Actually Does

A drain snake, also called an auger, is a coil of steel cable with a corkscrew tip. When you turn the handle, the tip bores into a clog and either breaks it apart or snags it so you can pull it out. It works best on soft blockages such as grease, food scraps and hair mats. It does not work well on a full blockage of solid material or on a pipe that has collapsed.

A kitchen sink is more complicated than a bathroom sink. The pipe under it has a trap, which is a U-shaped bend that holds water to block sewer gas. Many kitchens also have a garbage disposal, a dishwasher drain connection and a double bowl that joins into one line. Each of those adds a place for a clog and a place that can make snaking harder.

Before You Snake Anything

  • ✓Stop running water into the sink and let standing water settle.
  • ✓Check the disposal. If it hums but does not spin, it is jammed. Turn it off at the switch and do not put your hand in.
  • ✓Do not mix chemical drain cleaners with a snake. The chemicals can splash back and burn skin.
  • ✓Clear the cabinet under the sink, set out a bucket and old towels, and wear gloves and eye protection.
  • ✓Check whether the dishwasher drains into this sink. A dishwasher that will not drain is often the first sign.

Snaking the Sink Step by Step

1

Remove the trap

Place the bucket under the trap, loosen the slip nuts by hand or with pliers and pull the trap. Dump it and check for debris. Often that is the clog.

2

Feed the cable

Push the snake into the wall stub, not the sink drain, and feed it until you feel resistance.

3

Turn and push

Tighten the thumbscrew and crank clockwise while pushing gently. Let the cable do the work.

4

Pull and flush

Pull the cable out slowly, clean it, reassemble the trap and run hot water for a few minutes.

Do not force the cable. If it will not advance, or if it advances a great distance with no clog, the problem may be farther down. Back it out and stop.

When Snaking Is Not Enough

A snake opens a hole. If grease coats the pipe, a snake can punch through and leave a narrow channel that clogs again in weeks. A repeat clog usually means grease or scale on the pipe wall, or a low spot that holds water. Hydro jetting washes the whole pipe wall, and our hydro jetting page explains when it helps.

Local Note: Nashua Kitchens and the Sewer

Nashua is on municipal sewer, and the city operates a wastewater treatment plant, according to the New Hampshire Employment Security community profile. That means a kitchen sink clog that stays within your own pipes is yours to clear, while a blockage in the city main is the city to deal with. A snake that runs freely through your branch line and still leaves a backup points the finger at the main.

In Hudson and Merrimack, the mix of municipal and private septic means the same symptom can have a different cause. A kitchen line that drains slowly on a septic system can mean a full tank, so always ask before you assume a clog.

What Not to Put Down a Kitchen Sink

Grease is the main enemy. Pouring cooking fat down the sink feels harmless because it is liquid when hot. It cools and sticks to the pipe wall. Coffee grounds, eggshells and starchy foods such as rice and pasta also build up. Scrape plates into the trash, wipe pans with a paper towel and use a strainer.

Why Kitchen Sinks Clog Again After Snaking

A kitchen line is the hardest working drain in the house. Cooking fat, starch from pasta water and ground food scraps all pass through it, and the pipe is narrow, often only an inch and a half. After a snake punches through, a thin film of grease remains on the wall. Over weeks that film catches new scraps and the line narrows again.

If the same sink slows within a month, stop snaking and change the approach. Run very hot tap water for a minute after washing greasy pans, keep a strainer in the drain and wipe fat from pans before washing. If the pattern continues, the line probably needs a full cleaning with a jetter or a look with a camera to find a low spot or damaged section.

Double Sinks, Dishwashers and Disposals

A double bowl joins into one tailpiece, and the dishwasher often drains into the disposal or a separate fitting. If the water backs up from one bowl into the other, the blockage sits past the tee. If the dishwasher fills the sink at the end of a cycle, check the air gap or high loop and the knockout plug inside the disposal inlet, which is a common cause of a dishwasher that will not drain after a new disposal is installed.
