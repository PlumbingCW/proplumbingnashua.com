Planning a Water Heater System Install in Nashua, NH

A good water heater installation starts before the delivery truck. Measure the space, confirm your fuel and venting, decide how many gallons or how much flow you need, and check what the city requires. That planning decides whether install day goes smoothly or turns into a second visit.

By the Nashua Pro Plumbing team. Published November 3, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

Start With the Room

Walk to the water heater and look around it. Where does it vent? Where does the gas line or the electrical feed come from? Is there a floor drain nearby? How much clearance is there on each side? A new unit may be taller, wider or need more room around it than the old one.

  • ✓Measure the height, width and depth of the space.
  • ✓Note the fuel, gas or electric, and the flue size if gas.
  • ✓Photograph the labels on the old tank.
  • ✓Check for a floor drain or a drain pan.
  • ✓Clear a path from the door to the heater.

Decide the Size and Type

Tank size follows the household. A household of two may be well served by a modest tank, while a family of five with teenagers needs more recovery. Fuel affects both cost and recovery speed. Gas tanks heat water quickly. Electric tanks recover slower. A heat pump hybrid is efficient but needs space and air. A tankless unit gives continuous hot water but needs gas supply or electrical capacity to match.

Venting, Gas and Electrical

Venting is a safety issue. A gas water heater must exhaust combustion gases outdoors through a flue sized and pitched for the unit. Old flues can corrode or sit at the wrong angle. Replacing the tank is a good time to inspect the flue and fix it. Gas lines and electrical circuits should match the new unit.

For electrical work, call a licensed electrician if the circuit or breaker has to change. For gas work, use a licensed plumber or gas fitter. New Hampshire licenses plumbers and gas fitters through the Mechanical Safety and Licensing Board.

Local Note: Nashua Code and Water

The City of Nashua adopts the International Plumbing Code under the New Hampshire State Building Code with local amendments. Those amendments address water heater storage temperature and tempering for tankless and faucet water. Ask the Department of Building Safety whether your installation needs a permit.

Pennichuck reports soft water for the Nashua system, near 21 ppm hardness, and a pH above 8, so tanks face less scale here than in hard water areas. In Hudson, where town water tests harder, the tank may need flushing more often. In Hollis, well water varies, so test it before you pick a heater or a softener.

Sources: City of Nashua plumbing code (ecode360), Pennichuck Water Works Consumer Confidence Report, NH Office of Professional Licensure and Certification.

After the Install

Ask the installer to show you the temperature setting, the relief valve and the shutoffs. Write down the date and the model number. Schedule a flush at the interval that matches your water. If your old tank failed suddenly, read our water heater replacement page for the emergency path.

Questions to Ask Your Installer

  • ✓Will you size the tank or unit for my household, and how?
  • ✓What venting or gas line changes does the new unit need?
  • ✓Will you pull the permit if one is required, and who schedules the inspection?
  • ✓What is included, such as a new relief valve, discharge pipe, expansion tank and flexible connectors?
  • ✓How do you dispose of the old unit?
  • ✓What is the maintenance schedule for the new unit?
  • ✓What happens if something is found after the old tank comes out?

A clear answer to each question is a sign of a careful installer. A vague answer means the quote may change on install day.

Common Surprises on Install Day

The most common surprises are old shutoff valves that will not close, a flue that no longer meets current practice, a gas connector that needs replacing, and corrosion on nearby pipes. None of these is unusual in an older house. We check them when we look at the heater and tell you before the day arrives. If a shutoff valve fails, we replace it, so the next job is simpler.

A planned install also lets you decide on extras, such as an expansion tank in a closed system, a drain pan with a drain line to a floor drain, or a leak sensor that alerts your phone. These add little to the job and protect the room if the tank ever fails.

Timing Your Installation

Replace a heater on your schedule, not the tank schedule. If your heater is old and has begun to show rust or noise, plan the replacement for a mild week rather than waiting for a failure on the coldest night. Order the unit ahead of time, line up the plumber and clear the space. You will avoid a rush job, have time to compare options and often avoid water damage from a tank that fails without warning.

The Short Version

Good installs come from good planning. Measure the room, confirm fuel and venting, size the unit for the household, ask the building office about permits and pick a date before the old tank fails. Then clear the space, find your shutoffs and ask the installer to walk you through the settings when the work is done.
