Emergency Toilet Repair in Nashua, NH

A toilet that will not stop running, flushes weakly, leaks at the base or has stopped working altogether. We repair what can be repaired and install a new one when that makes more sense.

If a Toilet Is Overflowing

Our 24/7 line is open, so call when a toilet will not stop. First, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water. Do not flush again. Use towels to keep water off the floor and keep the bathroom door closed if sewage is involved.

A toilet that overflows and a second fixture that gurgles point to a main line issue, not a toilet issue. Tell us that on the phone. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and a toilet that cannot be used is the kind of call we try to fit in.

Toilet Installation and Replacement

Sometimes repair does not make sense. A cracked bowl, a repeatedly leaking base on a failing flange or an old high-flow unit may be worth replacing. We remove the old toilet, check the flange and floor, set a new toilet on a fresh seal, and test it.

  • ✓We check the flange height and condition before setting the new toilet.
  • ✓We use a new wax or wax-free seal and new bolts.
  • ✓We level the toilet and check for rocking.
  • ✓We test the flush and look for leaks before we leave.

If the floor around the toilet is soft or the flange is broken, we tell you and explain what repair is needed. Rot under a toilet is not a cosmetic problem.

Toilets in Nashua Homes

Older Nashua homes in Downtown, French Hill and the Tree Streets may have had plumbing changed many times, so we check the toilet, the flange and the drain before recommending anything. A slow toilet may be the first sign of a partial clog or a drain problem, and in any home the culprit can be wipes or a toy.

Pennichuck water is soft, with a reported hardness of about 21 ppm, so mineral buildup in toilet tanks is lower than in hard water areas. In Hudson and on private wells in Hollis, minerals can foul fill valves and rim jets faster, and iron can stain bowls. We account for that when we choose parts.

Backups and Septic Systems

In Hollis and other septic areas, a toilet that backs up repeatedly can mean a full tank or a failing leach field. Do not keep flushing. Call us, and we check the line and the tank. A septic problem needs the right fix, and plunging a toilet harder will not solve it.

Water Efficiency Note

If your toilet is old and runs often, a repair may pay off more than a swap. When a replacement is the better choice, the EPA WaterSense label identifies models that use 1.28 gallons or less per flush and are tested for performance.
